Welcome — take a few minutes for yourself.

Studying takes concentration, and staying in one position for a long time can leave you feeling mentally tired or physically stiff. Short breaks give you a chance to change position, move, rest your eyes, and return to your work feeling more settled.

01 · Mindful breathing

Try extended-exhale breathing

This is a simple four-part breathing pattern with a longer, slower exhale. The exhale is longer than the inhale; keep every breath comfortable and unforced.

1 · Inhale — 4 secondsBreathe in gently through your nose.
2 · Pause — 2 secondsRest briefly, only if comfortable.
3 · Exhale — 6 secondsSlowly breathe out, gently and without pushing.
4 · Pause — 2 secondsLet the next breath begin naturally.

Try 3–4 comfortable cycles. Stop or return to natural breathing at any time.

Keep it comfortable. Don't force a deep breath or hold your breath if it feels uncomfortable. Shorten the counts, skip the pauses, or return to normal breathing whenever you need to. Stop if you feel light-headed.

Mindfulness tip: Notice your feet on the floor. Let your attention return gently to your breathing whenever your mind wanders.

02 · Movement habit

Get up about every 45 minutes

Use 45 minutes as a helpful reminder to change position—not a strict rule. When your study timer ends, take 2–5 minutes to move in a way that feels good.

WalkTake a short lap around your room or hallway.
HydrateStand up and refill your water bottle.
Look awayRest your eyes by looking at something farther away.
Change positionStand, reach overhead, or gently roll your shoulders.

Make it easy: Set a recurring 45-minute study timer. When it goes off, save your work, stand up, and take a short movement break.

03 · Neck & shoulder mobility

Four gentle desk-friendly movements

Move slowly, stay within a comfortable range, and keep your shoulders relaxed. You should feel a gentle stretch—not pain.

  1. Side neck stretch: Sit tall. Gently tilt one ear toward the same-side shoulder without lifting the shoulder. Hold 10–15 seconds, then switch sides.
  2. Back-of-neck / levator stretch: Turn your head about 45 degrees, then gently look down toward your armpit. Hold 10–15 seconds, then switch sides.
  3. Chin tucks: Look straight ahead and glide your chin gently backward, as if making a small double chin. Keep your head level. Hold 3–5 seconds; repeat 5–8 times.
  4. Shoulder rolls: Slowly roll both shoulders up, back, and down 8–10 times. Keep the movement smooth and easy.
Safety first: Don't pull on your head, force the range, or make fast neck circles. Stop if a movement causes pain, dizziness, tingling, numbness, or other unusual symptoms. If you have a neck injury or a condition that affects movement, ask a qualified healthcare professional for guidance before trying the stretches.
04 · Your 3-minute reset

Put it all together

  1. Pause (30 seconds): Save your work, put both feet on the floor, and relax your shoulders.
  2. Breathe (60 seconds): Try a few comfortable rounds of extended-exhale breathing—or simply breathe naturally.
  3. Move (60 seconds): Stand up, walk a little, and do a few shoulder rolls.
  4. Stretch (30 seconds): Try a gentle side neck stretch on each side, only if comfortable.

Adjust the timing to suit your needs. The goal is a brief, comfortable change of pace—not to complete every movement perfectly.

05 · Make it a habit

Your next study session

Choose one small action you can try today:

  • Set a reminder to stand and move about every 45 minutes.
  • Try a few comfortable extended-exhale breaths when you want to pause.
  • Keep the neck and shoulder movements handy for your next screen break.

Remember: A break isn't wasted study time. It's a chance to look after your body and give your attention a moment to reset.
